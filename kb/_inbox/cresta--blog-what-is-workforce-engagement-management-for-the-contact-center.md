# What Is Workforce Engagement Management for Contact Centers?

Content Updated of: July 2026

Picture a contact center where a third of your agents will be gone within the year. You hire, onboard, and rebuild tenure you just lost, over and over. This is the retention problem workforce engagement management is built to solve.

The churn is not rare. McKinsey found in 2020 that most contact centers see [**annual turnover of up to 60%**](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/operations/our-insights/customer-care-the-future-talent-factory), with best-in-class centers near 20%. More recent tracking by Metrigy put agent turnover at [**28.1% in 2023, projected to reach 31.2% in 2024**](https://www.techtarget.com/searchcustomerexperience/tip/Why-contact-centers-have-high-turnover-and-how-to-combat-it).

Operations and quality leaders keep asking the same questions:

- What is workforce engagement management, and how does it differ from workforce management (WFM)?
- Does it reduce attrition, or is it another wellness initiative?
- What should we measure to know it is working?

This guide answers each one. It explains what WEM is, how it differs from WFM, and the core components of a WEM program. It also shows how Cresta uses Customer Experience AI to turn engagement into measurable outcomes.

The short version: WEM is the discipline of keeping agents engaged, developed, and productive. The best programs now run on AI that reads every conversation instead of a sampled few. The rest of this guide breaks down each piece and shows how to measure whether it is working.

## What is workforce engagement management?

Workforce engagement management is a strategy for maximizing the engagement, productivity, and satisfaction of contact center employees.

It is often confused with workforce management, but the two solve different problems. WFM handles forecasting, scheduling, and adherence: the operational mechanics of staffing the floor. WEM is broader.

It adds quality management, performance management, coaching, and engagement programs. Its focus is the employee experience, not operational efficiency alone.

Put simply, WFM asks whether you have the right people in the right seats at the right time. WEM asks whether those people are equipped, developed, and motivated to do their best work once they are there.

That difference matters for contact centers because scheduling alone does not fix attrition. You can staff perfectly and still lose agents who feel undervalued or stuck. WEM treats the human side of the operation as a system you manage on purpose.

WEM may include elements like:

- Employee feedback and surveys
- Performance management
- Training and development
- Gamification
- Wellness programs

## Core components of workforce engagement management

A workforce engagement management program is usually built from five connected building blocks. Each one addresses a different part of the agent experience. Together, they form a system where measurement feeds development and development feeds better results.

- **Workforce management:** Forecasting, scheduling, and adherence so staffing matches demand.
- **Quality management:** Scoring conversations against defined standards to keep service consistent.
- **Performance management:** Setting goals, tracking KPIs, and reviewing results with agents.
- **Coaching:** Turning performance data into targeted, repeatable improvement for each agent.
- **Analytics and feedback:** Surveys, sentiment, and conversation data that show how engaged agents are and why.

## Why workforce engagement management makes sense for contact centers

Contact centers are high-stress environments with high turnover. WEM addresses the root causes directly rather than treating symptoms. The payoff shows up across four areas: retention, customer experience, productivity, and alignment.

### Enhanced employee satisfaction and retention

Engaged teams leave far less often. [**According to Gallup**](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/236927/employee-engagement-drives-growth.aspx), low-engagement teams see turnover rates **18% to 43% higher** than highly engaged teams. Giving agents feedback, growth, and recognition keeps more of them in their seats.

Retention compounds. Every agent you keep is one you do not have to recruit, onboard, and bring up to full productivity. The cost of that churn is the clearest financial case for investing in WEM.

### Improved customer experience

Engagement shows up in the customer relationship. In [Gallup's Q12 meta-analysis](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/321725/gallup-q12-meta-analysis-report.aspx), top-quartile engaged business units achieve **10% higher customer loyalty and engagement** than bottom-quartile units.

The mechanism is simple. Agents who feel invested in bring more patience and care to each conversation. Customers feel the difference, and it shows up in loyalty and repeat business.

### Increased productivity

Engaged agents bring more focus and discretionary effort to each interaction. That means faster resolutions and less rework, without pushing people toward burnout.

Productivity gains from engagement tend to last, because they come from motivation rather than pressure. Squeezing more calls out of a disengaged team plateaus quickly and often backfires through higher attrition.

### Tighter alignment with goals

WEM connects daily agent behavior to broader business goals. When agents see how their work maps to outcomes like retention and CSAT, they make better decisions in the moment.

Alignment also gives coaching a clear target. Instead of coaching to generic scripts, leaders can coach to the specific behaviors that move the metrics the business cares about most.

## How to measure workforce engagement

You measure workforce engagement with a small set of metrics tracked continuously, not once a year. The goal is a live read on how agents feel and how that connects to performance. Leaders typically watch:

- **Employee Net Promoter Score (eNPS):** How likely agents are to recommend your center as a place to work.
- **Voluntary turnover / attrition rate:** The share of agents who leave by choice.
- **Schedule adherence:** How closely agents follow their planned schedules.
- **CSAT correlation:** How agent engagement tracks against customer satisfaction scores.
- **Pulse-survey cadence:** Frequency of short, continuous surveys rather than one annual survey.

No single number tells the whole story. Read them together: rising eNPS with falling attrition and steady CSAT is the pattern that signals a WEM program is working. Watching them continuously lets you act on a downward trend before it becomes a wave of resignations.
