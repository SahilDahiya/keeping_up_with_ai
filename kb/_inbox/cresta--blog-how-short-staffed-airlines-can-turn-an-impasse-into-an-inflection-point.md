# How Short-Staffed Airlines Can Turn the Tide

The last couple of years have been a bumpy ride for airlines. Demand for air travel [**fell by over 65%**](https://airlines.iata.org/2021/02/10/catastrophic-year-air-travel-demand) in 2020, leading to [**hundreds of thousands of job losses**](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jackkelly/2021/02/01/airlines-lost-over-40000-workers-united-airlines-announced-another-14000-jobs-may-be-lost/?sh=1a9860c924b3) in the industry. But as [**passenger volume increases**](https://www.tsa.gov/coronavirus/passenger-throughput) progressively, meeting (and even exceeding) pre-pandemic numbers, times are still turbulent for airlines.Now attempting to bounce back from job cuts, airlines are struggling to re-staff across the board — a fact that has far-reaching implications. So, with headcount low and demand high, airlines somehow need to create positive customer experiences while maintaining employee satisfaction in their contact centers.

## **Short Staff, Long Wait Times**

Most airlines made major cuts in 2020. Now, as demand for air travel bounces back, the impact of those cuts is being felt acutely by customers and staff alike. [**Lacking headcount**](https://www.businessinsider.com/airlines-labor-shortage-cancelling-flights-aviation-jobs-market-2022-6) in every area — from pilots and flight attendants to baggage handlers and agents — airlines have canceled [**11% more flights**](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/us-flight-cancellations-delays-this-year-surpass-2019-levels-data-2022-08-12/) so far in 2022 than during the same period in 2019. This means that irregular operations (IROPs) and their corresponding volume spikes are happening more frequently, and airline agents are struggling to keep up. [**Excessive wait times**](https://www.washingtonpost.com/travel/tips/airline-customer-service-hold-times/) to reach airline customer service have been top of mind since 2021 and it’s only gotten worse — the public has become [**increasingly critical of airline customer service**](https://www.wsj.com/articles/annoying-airline-wait-times-arent-going-away-this-summer-11656469942) as customers share more and more horror stories of spending hours on hold. All of this may account for why the DOT has received [**300% more consumer complaints**](https://www.transportation.gov/briefing-room/air-travel-consumer-report-consumer-complaints-against-airlines-rise-more-300-percent) about airlines in 2022 than they did pre-pandemic. So as airlines [**struggle to staff**](https://www.reuters.com/world/the-great-reboot/us-airlines-scramble-find-call-center-staff-travel-returns-2021-05-19/) their contact centers, agents’ risk for burnout is higher than ever. 

## **More Work on Top of More Work**

At the most basic level, there are two distinct challenges at play in [airline contact centers](https://cresta.com/airlines/): headcount is down and volume is up. In combination, these issues make each other exponentially worse. To put it in context, some airlines cut their staff by 50% during the pandemic. That means agents would still be handling up to double the work they had in 2019 if the volume of conversations only bounced back to 100% of what it was pre-pandemic. But we know that volume hasn’t *just* recovered — it has increased. Thanks to staffing shortages in every area turning IROPs into a regular occurrence, agents are handling significantly more conversations with more frustrated customers and fewer resources. With the [**outlook on regaining headcount**](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/business-news/american-airlines-says-take-3-years-get-back-full-nationwide-capacity-rcna39538) rather bleak, airlines must keep their agents happy and somehow make them more efficient. Simply put, employees are fewer, complaints are higher, and customer loyalty is more important than ever — airlines need to find a way to do more with less. 

## **Key Tactics to Enable Agent Efficiency**

With so many evolving challenges threatening the industry, why should airlines focus on improving agent efficiency? Because as the industry’s hiring frenzy creeps along, it’s the most impactful step airlines can take *right now.* In this critical period, airlines can help boost efficiency and make agents’ jobs easier while better serving clients. Cutting costs is an oft-cited objective, so more efficient agents bring immense value. There are a few issues that contribute to a lack of efficiency within airline contact centers:

- Volume is too high relative to staff
- Agents lack up-to-date information during IROPs
- Information relevant to complex customer issues is difficult to access
- Rote, administrative tasks like note taking require too much time
