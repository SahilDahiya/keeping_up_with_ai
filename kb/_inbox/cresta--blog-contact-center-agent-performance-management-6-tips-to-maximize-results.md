# 6 Tips to Maximize Contact Center Agent Performance

*Updated July 23, 2026*

Which agent [behaviors move CSAT the most](https://cresta.com/guides/how-to-improve-csat)? How do you coach every agent, not just a sampled few? And how do you close the performance gap without piling more manual work onto stretched teams? These are the questions operations and quality leaders ask when contact center agent performance decides whether targets are hit or missed.

Customers expect more from every interaction, and CX is now a competitive battleground. Effective performance management drives customer satisfaction, loyalty, and operational efficiency. But as interaction volume rises and the gap between top and bottom agents stays wide, leaders can feel too buried in daily demands to manage performance proactively. Below are six actionable tips to strengthen your contact center agent performance management strategy.

## Key Takeaways

- Set clear, measurable goals and align KPIs like AHT, FCR, and CSAT with business outcomes.
- Train continuously and pair training with real-time guidance so newer agents reach proficiency faster.
- Monitor performance as it happens instead of sampling calls days or weeks later.
- Use AI-driven analytics across 100% of conversations to find the behaviors that drive outcomes.
- Prioritize employee experience, since engaged teams see **78%** lower absenteeism (Gallup).
- Automate coaching and feedback so every agent gets consistent, in-the-moment input.

## 1. Set Clear, Measurable Goals And KPIs

To raise performance, agents need to know which outcomes matter and how success is measured. Key performance indicators (KPIs) worth tracking include average handle time (AHT), first call resolution (FCR), customer satisfaction (CSAT), QA score, and conversion rate. For a fuller list, see our guide to the [15 critical contact center KPIs](https://cresta.com/blog/the-15-critical-contact-center-kpis). Whichever you choose, the contact center's goals should align with larger business objectives.

This is harder than it sounds. According to Gallup (2024), only [**2% of Fortune 500 CHROs**](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/644717/chros-think-performance-management-system-works.aspx) strongly agree their performance management system inspires employees to improve. To get the most from your performance management efforts, start by setting and communicating clear goals.

## 2. Provide Continuous Training And Development

Equipping agents to keep their skills sharp is central to thoughtful performance management. This can include regular training, workshops, or one-to-one coaching on both soft skills (empathy, assuming the sale) and technical skills (new software, specific objections).

Continuous training paired with real-time guidance helps newer agents reach proficiency faster, and the gains are largest for less-experienced agents. A shorter ramp means every agent reaches strong performance sooner, without waiting months to contribute.

## 3. Implement Real-Time Performance Monitoring

Outdated [quality management](https://cresta.com/cresta-quality-management) methods like call sampling are slow and largely ineffective. Too often, agents are coached on conversations from days or weeks ago, when the details are hard to recall. Sampling also reviews only a small fraction of interactions.

[Real-time performance monitoring tools](https://cresta.com/cresta-insights) let managers track performance as it happens, without extra manual work. ## 5. Prioritize Employee Experience

Employee experience is overlooked in many performance plans, yet a positive, supportive environment lifts agent performance. Gallup's Q12 Meta-Analysis (2024) found highly engaged business units see [**78% lower absenteeism and 23% higher profitability**](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/321725/gallup-q12-meta-analysis-report.aspx) than the least engaged.

Put simply, [happier agents perform better](https://cresta.com/blog/a-three-phased-guide-to-happier-agents/). To address employee experience, provide personalized coaching so agents feel supported, add gamification to daily work, and invest in tools that offload the rote tasks that drive burnout.
